Lab-grown vs. natural diamonds: the honest comparison

One is pulled from the ground, one is grown above it — and on your hand, no gemologist can tell them apart without a machine. Here's the whole picture, including the resale part most sellers skip.

Are they real? (Yes — settled.)

A lab-grown diamond is carbon in the same crystal structure as a mined one: same 10-on-Mohs hardness, same fire and brilliance, same chemistry. The FTC calls them diamonds. IGI and GIA grade them on the same 4C scale. They are not cubic zirconia and not moissanite — those are different materials. The only reliable way to tell lab from mined is specialized lab equipment reading trace growth patterns.

The resale truth (from someone not selling you either)

Neither diamond is an investment. Natural diamonds resell for a fraction of what retail charged; lab-grown resale is weaker still because production keeps getting cheaper. If a salesperson leads with "holds its value," walk. The honest frame: you're buying something to be worn and loved for decades — put the budget into the stone on the hand, not a resale story.

Who should buy which

  • Lab-grown: you want maximum stone for the budget, a guaranteed-conflict-free origin, or a bigger/cleaner stone than mined pricing allows. This is now the majority of engagement-ring stones sold in the US.
  • Natural: the billion-year origin story genuinely matters to you, you're buying estate/heirloom pieces, or tradition is the point. That's a valid reason — it's just a sentiment purchase, not a financial one.

Buying either one: the checklist

  1. Independent certificate (IGI or GIA), and the laser inscription on the girdle matches it.
  2. Cut over carat — cut quality drives sparkle more than size does.
  3. See it under normal light, not just jewelry-store spotlights — a local jeweler beats a website here.
  4. Ask the return and upgrade policy before, not after.
