Window tint percentages, explained — and what's legal in Washington

"35%" doesn't mean 35% dark — it means 35% of light gets through. Here's what each percentage actually looks like, and where Washington draws the legal line.

What VLT percentages mean

Tint is measured in VLT — visible light transmission. The number is how much light passes through: lower number, darker window.

Washington's rules in plain English

  • Passenger cars: every window behind the windshield must let through at least 24% of light.
  • Windshield: no tint film except a non-reflective band on the top six inches.
  • Reflectivity: film can't reflect more than 35% of light — mirror tint is out.
  • SUVs, vans, trucks (multipurpose vehicles): windows behind the driver may be darker than 24%, as long as the vehicle has both side mirrors.
  • Medical exemption: darker tint is allowed with a licensed physician's documentation for light-sensitive conditions — keep it in the car.
Check before you install: this summarizes RCW 46.37.430 as of August 2026. Laws change and enforcement varies — a reputable shop will refuse an illegal install on a passenger car rather than sell you a fix-it ticket.

Frequently asked

Is 20% tint legal in Washington?

On a passenger car's side or rear windows — no, the floor is 24% VLT. On an SUV, van, or truck, 20% (or darker) is fine on windows behind the driver.

Will I get pulled over for dark tint?

Over-dark tint on a passenger car is a traffic infraction and a common add-on citation. Shops that install it anyway are trading your ticket for their sale.

Does ceramic tint change the legal math?

No — the law only cares about VLT and reflectivity. A 70% ceramic film blocks most heat and UV while staying nearly clear, which is why it's the go-to for windshields.
