# Pricing, derived from the operator's economics

Not "what feels sellable" — what the math supports, so it holds up on a call
when an owner pushes back.

## What a bed is worth (the only number that matters)

Anchored on Serene: a private-pay resident is ~$5,000/month. California,
Arizona and Florida board-and-care private pay runs roughly $3,500–6,000.

| | |
|---|---|
| Full 6-bed home, private pay | $30,000/mo · **$360,000/yr** gross |
| **One empty bed** | **−$5,000/mo · −$60,000/yr** |
| One placement, 20-month stay | **$100,000** lifetime to the operator |
| Owner's net (≈25% margin, owner-operated) | ~$7,500/mo |

The service doesn't compete against other marketing budgets. It competes
against **an empty room**, which costs $5,000 a month whether or not anyone
markets anything.

## The competitor anchor nobody can argue with

A placement agency charges roughly **one month's rent per placement** —
about $5,000, every family, every time, forever. That is the price the
operator already accepts as normal.

> "A Place for Mom charges you a month's rent for one resident — about
> $5,000. That's a year of us. And we don't stop at one."

That single comparison does more work than any feature list.

## Price as a share of value

| Price | Per year | Share of ONE bed's annual revenue | In resident-days/month |
|---|---|---|---|
| $400/mo | $4,800 | 8% | 2.4 days |
| $500/mo | $6,000 | 10% | 3.0 days |
| $750/mo | $9,000 | 15% | 4.5 days |
| $1,000/mo | $12,000 | 20% | 6.0 days |

Best framing on a call: **"less than three days of one resident's rate,
per month."**

## The affordability ceiling

Against the owner's *net profit* (~$7,500/mo on a full 6-bed home):

- $400/mo = **5%** of net → easy yes
- $750/mo = **10%** of net → yes with a clear ROI story
- $1,000/mo = **13%** of net → painful for a 6-bed home, invites churn
- $1,300/mo (RareCurve's Pro) → only sane for 12+ beds or multi-home owners

A 6-bed home is not a med spa. $1,000/mo is priced for a business twice its
size, and price-driven churn is the one kind you can't fix with better work.

## CAC is not the constraint

At ~$250 CAC via cold email and ~$150/client/month delivery cost:

| Scenario | Gross profit | LTV:CAC |
|---|---|---|
| $400/mo, 24-month tenure | $7,500 | **30×** |
| $750/mo, 24-month tenure | $15,900 | **64×** |
| $400/mo, 12-month tenure (pessimistic) | $4,500 | **18×** |

Healthy threshold is 3×. RareCurve runs 2.3×. Every scenario here clears it
by an order of magnitude, and the build fee alone repays CAC on day one —
versus RareCurve's 4.7-month payback.

**So price is not the broken lever.** With CAC this low, the binding
constraints are **delivery capacity** and **retention**, which is what the
tiering below is actually solving for.

## Recommended structure

**The Build — $1,500 one-time** (founding rate $1,000 for the first five per
state). Website, Google Business Profile, call tracking; photography included
in Washington, guided DIY elsewhere. Registered in their name, theirs
forever. Waive $250 on a 6-month prepay to pull cash forward.

**The Fill — monthly, scaled to licensed beds**, because value scales with beds:

| Tier | Licensed beds | Price | Typical operator |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home | ≤6 | **$400/mo** | the classic AFH / 6-bed board and care |
| Home+ | 7–15 | **$750/mo** | larger RCFE, CBRF, Type B ALF |
| Portfolio | 16+ or multi-home | **$1,200/mo** | 2–3 homes under one owner |

Month-to-month after a 3-month start. Cancel anytime, keep everything.

Optional **Ads** add-on at +$500/mo plus their own ad spend — only for homes
with two or more beds open that want them filled fast.

## Why per-bed beats one flat price

- It's defensible out loud: the same 8–10% of one bed's value at every size.
- It stops you underpricing a 16-bed home at $400 and overpricing a 4-bed at $1,000.
- It creates natural expansion revenue: a client who opens a second home moves
  up a tier without renegotiation.

## What this means for the target list

Solo delivery capacity is roughly 15–25 clients. So:

| Path to $10K MRR | Clients needed | Delivery load |
|---|---|---|
| all Home tier ($400) | 25 | at the solo ceiling |
| all Home+ ($750) | **13** | comfortable |
| all Portfolio ($1,200) | 8 | easy |

**Bias the outreach toward larger and multi-home operators.** One Portfolio
client is worth three Home clients at a third of the overhead. The spine
already carries review count and name patterns; multi-home owners are
identifiable and should sort to the top of the call list.

Also refine the ICP toward **private-pay homes**. Medicaid-heavy homes bill
roughly $2,500–3,500/bed, run thinner margins, and get residents through case
managers rather than search — less money and less need. Private-pay homes
have both.

## The close

> "One empty bed costs you $5,000 a month. This costs $400 — less than three
> days of one resident's rate. If it fills one bed for one month this year, it
> paid for itself twice over. And if it doesn't work, fire me: the website and
> the Google profile are in your name and you keep them."
