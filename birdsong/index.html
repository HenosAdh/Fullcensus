Memory care and dementia care in Kent

Memory care

The same day, every day, on purpose. Same faces, same rhythm, meals at the same table. When someone cannot hold on to the last hour, a predictable day is what keeps them calm.

Dementia care

Six residents, low noise, nothing startling. We watch for the small changes that come before the big ones, and we tell families early.

Behavioral support

Most agitation has a reason: pain, noise, tiredness, a change in routine. We look for the reason before we respond to the behavior.

Respite care and short stays

Surgery, a trip, or simply a week of rest. Call and we will work it out. Families come back to us for this again and again.

Medications

Every dose on time and written down. Refills handled, appointments coordinated with each resident's own doctors.

Everyday living

Bathing, dressing, grooming, getting around safely. Home-cooked meals, clean rooms, laundry done, and company through the day.

Questions families ask

The things people want to know

What is an adult family home in Washington state?

An adult family home is a private residence, licensed by Washington State DSHS, caring for up to six residents. It provides room, board, laundry, supervision and help with daily living. Because the numbers are small, residents get far more individual attention than a large facility can offer.

What is the difference between an adult family home and assisted living?

Assisted living is usually a large building of private apartments, often a hundred or more, with staff available as needed. An adult family home is a real house with a maximum of six residents. The care is closer, the same caregivers are there every day, and meals are cooked in the kitchen and eaten at one table.

For someone living with dementia, that consistency usually matters more than amenities do.

Who are adult family homes for?

Adults who can no longer safely live on their own. That includes older adults with dementia or memory loss, people who need help with bathing, dressing and medications, and people whose needs are too high for independent living but who do not need a nursing home. Birdsong specializes in memory loss and dementia.

How much does an adult family home in Kent cost?

It depends on the level of care a person needs and whether the room is private or shared, so there is no single number we could publish that would be honest. Call Mel on (253) 740-0890 and she will tell you plainly what it would cost for your situation.

Do you accept Medicaid?

Yes. Birdsong accepts private pay and Medicaid, and we hold an Expanded Community Services contract, which covers residents whose needs are higher than a standard placement. If you are working with a DSHS case manager, they can contact Mel directly.

Do you offer respite care or short stays?

Yes. If you care for someone at home and need a short stay covered, for surgery, a trip, or simply to rest, call and we will talk it through. Families come back to us for this again and again.

How many residents live at Birdsong?

Up to six, which is the maximum a Washington adult family home license allows. Keeping the home small is exactly what makes the individual attention possible.

Which areas do you serve?

Birdsong is on East Hill in Kent. Families come to us from Kent, Renton, Auburn, Covington, Federal Way, Des Moines, Tukwila, Maple Valley and across south King County.

Can we come and see the home first?

Please do. Visits are welcome by appointment. Call Mel on (253) 740-0890 and she will find a time that suits your family.
