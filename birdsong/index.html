What is an adult family home?

An adult family home is an ordinary house, licensed by Washington State DSHS, where up to six residents live and are cared for. Not a wing of a building. An actual home, on an actual street.

Because the number is capped at six, an adult family home can give a kind of attention a large facility cannot. The same caregivers every day. Meals cooked in the kitchen and eaten at one table. Someone who notices when a person is quieter than usual.

People often compare adult family homes with assisted living. Assisted living is usually a large building of private apartments with staff on call. An AFH is a house with six people in it. For someone living with dementia, that difference — familiar faces, a small quiet space, an unchanging routine — tends to matter more than amenities do.

Birdsong is licensed for six, and we have been doing this on East Hill in Kent since 2007.

Our care

Memory care and dementia care in Kent

Birdsong is the home we set up for memory loss. Choose any of these to see how we handle it.

Memory care

The day runs the same way every day, on purpose. The same faces, the same rhythm, meals at the same table. When someone cannot hold on to what happened an hour ago, a predictable day is the thing that makes them feel safe. We do not correct or argue. We redirect gently and move on.

Dementia care

Birdsong is our dementia home and the house is set up for it. Six residents, low noise, nothing startling. We watch for the small changes that come before the big ones, and we tell families early rather than waiting for something to go wrong.

Behavioral support

A calm, low-stimulation household, and a team who look for what sits behind a behavior before responding to it. Most agitation has a reason: pain, noise, tiredness, a change in routine. We look for the reason first.

Respite care and short stays

Short stays are welcome. If you care for someone at home and you need a week for surgery, for a trip, or simply to rest, call and we will talk through what is possible. Families come back to us for this again and again.

Medications and health

Every dose given on time and recorded, refills handled, and appointments coordinated with each resident's own doctors. We work alongside the visiting nurse and the wider care team so nothing falls between people.

Everyday living

Gentle help with bathing, dressing, grooming and getting around safely. Home-cooked meals shared at the table. Clean rooms, laundry taken care of, and company through the day, which matters as much as any of the rest of it.

The path to the door is lined with dogwood and roses. It is a small thing, but it is the first thing a family sees, and it tells them what sort of place this is before anybody says a word.

Meals are made fresh in the kitchen and eaten together. The clock, the calendar and the day's date are on the wall where everyone can see them, because for someone with memory loss those small anchors do real work.

Their own room

Each resident has their own space, and we encourage families to bring whatever makes it theirs. Photographs, a favorite chair, the blanket from home. It helps more than people expect it to.

The things people want to know

What is an adult family home in Washington state?

An adult family home is a private residence, licensed by Washington State DSHS, caring for up to six residents. It provides room, board, laundry, supervision and help with daily living. Because the numbers are small, residents get far more individual attention than a large facility can offer.

What is the difference between an adult family home and assisted living?

Assisted living is usually a large building of private apartments, often a hundred or more, with staff available as needed. An adult family home is a real house with a maximum of six residents. The care is closer, the same caregivers are there every day, and meals are cooked in the kitchen and eaten at one table.

For someone living with dementia, that consistency usually matters more than amenities do.

Who are adult family homes for?

Adults who can no longer safely live on their own. That includes older adults with dementia or memory loss, people who need help with bathing, dressing and medications, and people whose needs are too high for independent living but who do not need a nursing home. Birdsong specializes in memory loss and dementia.

Yes. Birdsong accepts private pay and Medicaid, and we hold an Expanded Community Services contract, which covers residents whose needs are higher than a standard placement. If you are working with a DSHS case manager, they can contact Mel directly.

Do you offer respite care or short stays?

Yes. If you care for someone at home and need a short stay covered, for surgery, a trip, or simply to rest, call and we will talk it through. Families come back to us for this again and again.

How many residents live at Birdsong?

Up to six, which is the maximum a Washington adult family home license allows. Keeping the home small is exactly what makes the individual attention possible.
