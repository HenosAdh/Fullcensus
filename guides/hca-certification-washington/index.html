Home Care Aide certification in Washington

Every caregiver you hire has two clocks running from their first day: 120 days to finish the training, 200 days to be certified. Miss either and you have a compliance problem and a staffing problem at the same time. Here is the whole thing, plainly.

Written by Henos Adhana, Full Census · My family runs an adult family home in Lynnwood · Updated August 2026 · Confirm current requirements with DOH and DSHS

The short version

  • Who needs it. Long-term care workers, which explicitly includes adult family home caregivers, unless they are exempt.
  • 75 hours of training through a DSHS-approved program.
  • Two deadlines from date of hire. Training done within 120 calendar days. Certified within 200 days.
  • An exam, administered by Prometric for the Department of Health.
  • A fingerprint background check through DSHS.
  • Fees are paid to DOH with the application, plus the Prometric exam fee. Both are non-refundable.
  • You, the owner, need this too as part of licensing, unless exempt.

Why this is the owner's problem, not the caregiver's

It is tempting to treat certification as something your caregiver sorts out on their own time. It is not. Those two deadlines start on their date of hire, which means they start on a date you chose. If somebody hits day 200 uncertified, that is your staffing gap, your compliance exposure, and your problem to solve mid-shift.

The homes that never have this problem do one thing differently: they put the two dates in a calendar on day one, and they check in at the halfway mark rather than the deadline.

Who counts as a long-term care worker

Broader than most owners assume. It includes home care agency caregivers, adult family home caregivers, respite care providers, individual providers contracting with DSHS for personal care, community residential service providers in group homes and supported living, and other direct care workers providing home or community-based services to elderly people or people with functional or developmental disabilities.

If somebody is delivering hands-on personal care in your home, assume they are in scope and check the exemptions rather than the other way round.

Exemptions exist, and you should confirm rather than assume. Certain workers, notably some licensed professionals such as nurses, are exempt from the home care aide requirement. Because getting this wrong means an uncertified caregiver on your floor, verify each person's status against the current DSHS and DOH guidance rather than relying on what somebody said at a training.

How this connects to your own licence

If you are opening a home rather than hiring for one, the same 75 hours applies to you. Washington expects the provider to complete the 75-hour orientation, safety and basic training plus Home Care Aide certification, unless exempt, alongside the separate 54-hour Administrator course.

Those are two different trainings and people conflate them constantly. The 75 hours makes you a certified caregiver. The 54 hours makes you a qualified administrator. You need both. The full licence requirements guide lays out where each one sits.

Keeping it from becoming a fire drill

  1. On day one, write both dates on the calendar: hire date plus 120 days, and hire date plus 200 days.
  2. Pick the DSHS-approved training programme before you hire, not after. Knowing where you send people removes the delay that eats the first month.
  3. Check in at day 60, halfway through the training window. This is the single habit that prevents the problem.
  4. Book the Prometric exam early. Seats are not infinite and a late booking can push somebody past day 200 through no fault of their own.
  5. Keep copies of everything in the employee file: training certificate, exam result, credential, background check. This is exactly what an inspector asks for.

Common questions

How many hours of training does a Home Care Aide need in Washington?

75 hours through a training program approved by the Department of Social and Health Services, followed by the home care aide certification examination.

How long does a caregiver have to get certified?

Long-term care workers must be certified as home care aides within 200 days of their date of hire, and must complete the required training within 120 calendar days of hire.

Do adult family home caregivers need Home Care Aide certification?

Yes. Adult family home caregivers are explicitly included in the definition of long-term care workers who must be certified, unless they fall under an exemption.

Who administers the Home Care Aide exam in Washington?

Prometric delivers the home care aide certification examination on behalf of the Washington State Department of Health.

How much does Home Care Aide certification cost?

There is a certification application fee payable to the Department of Health plus a Prometric examination fee, both due when the application is submitted and both non-refundable. Confirm the current amounts on the DOH fee schedule, as they change.

Is the 75-hour training the same as the 54-hour Administrator course?

No, and this is a common mix-up. The 75-hour training plus Home Care Aide certification is the caregiver credential. The 54-hour Adult Family Home Administrator course is a separate requirement for the person running the home. An owner-operator generally needs both.
