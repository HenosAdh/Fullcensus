What A Place for Mom actually costs a provider

It is free for families, and that is true. It is not free for you. The referral fee is reported at 85% to 100% of the resident’s first month of rent and care, with providers saying they have been asked for as much as 120%. On a $6,000 room that is $5,100 to $7,200, once, per resident.

Written by Henos Adhana, Full Census · My family runs an adult family home in Lynnwood · Updated August 2026 · We are not affiliated with A Place for Mom

The short version

  • Families pay nothing. The company is explicit about this, and it is genuinely true.
  • You pay the commission, and only when a resident actually moves in.
  • Reported range: 85% to 100% of the first month’s rent and care. Some providers report being asked for 90% to 120%.
  • Real numbers: single referrals have been reported around $3,500, and as high as $12,000 where dementia care pushes the monthly rate up.
  • Zero risk, high price. Nothing is owed unless they fill your bed, which is exactly why the fee is so large.
  • The honest use case: a bed that has been empty long enough that another vacant month costs more than the commission.

How the model works

A family calls, speaks to an advisor, and gets a shortlist of communities. That service costs the family nothing. The company earns when somebody moves in, and the payment comes from the care home.

That structure explains everything else about the experience. The advisor is choosing from homes inside a paying network, so being outside it means you are not in the conversation at all. And the fee is large because the company carries all the marketing cost and all the risk of a family who never moves.

The number that should focus your mind. A single dementia-care referral has been reported at up to $12,000. That is not an outrage in itself, because you only pay it when a bed is genuinely filled. But it is roughly twelve times what a website costs, and the website keeps working for years while the commission buys you exactly one resident.

When paying it is the right call

I am not going to tell you never to sign. Sometimes it is clearly correct:

  • The bed has been empty two months or more. You have already lost more than the fee. Filling it this week is the better trade, full stop.
  • You are brand new with no reputation and no reviews. An agency can bridge you to your first residents while your own presence gets built.
  • The placement is genuinely hard to match. Complex behavioural needs, bariatric care, or a situation where an advisor’s network beats a listing.

And when it is not: a bed that just opened, in a home families could have found on their own. Paying a full month of care to fill a room that would have filled in three weeks anyway is the most expensive invisible mistake in this business, because you never get to see the version where you kept the money.

One referral fee, or a website you own outright?

The commission on a single $6,000 room is roughly $5,100 to $7,200, once. A website with your home’s name on it costs a fraction of that, and it keeps bringing families every month afterwards. That is the whole argument.

Read the contract for these five things

  1. The exact percentage, and of what. First month’s rent only, or rent plus care fees? The difference is thousands.
  2. Exclusivity. Are you free to fill the same bed yourself while they are marketing it?
  3. The re-admission clause. If a resident leaves and returns, do you pay again?
  4. The attribution window. If a family contacts them, then finds you directly months later, is that still their commission?
  5. How to exit. Notice period, and whether fees survive termination for families already in the pipeline.

The part worth saying plainly

A referral agency’s incentive is to fill a bed, not necessarily your bed. The advisor is choosing among paying homes, and the ones that respond fastest and pay reliably tend to get shown first. That is not corruption, it is how every referral business works.

But it does mean you are renting access to families rather than building it. And anything you rent, you stop having the moment you stop paying. The homes that stay full year after year are the ones where families arrive already knowing the name, because a discharge planner recommended them or because they found the home themselves.

Common questions

How much does A Place for Mom charge a care home?

Reported at roughly 85% to 100% of the resident’s first month of rent and care, with some providers reporting requests of 90% to 120%. On a typical Washington adult family home room that is several thousand dollars, charged once, when the resident moves in.

Do families pay to use A Place for Mom?

No. The service is free to families. The care home pays the commission, which is why an advisor’s recommendations come from their network of paying communities.

Is A Place for Mom worth it for an adult family home?

It depends on how long the bed has been empty. If a room has sat vacant for two months you have already lost more than the commission, so paying it makes sense. For a bed that just opened in a home families can already find, it is usually the most expensive way to fill it.

Can I use A Place for Mom and still market my own home?

Usually yes, but read your agreement for exclusivity and attribution terms before assuming. Ask specifically whether a family who contacts them and later finds you directly still triggers a commission.

What is the cheapest way to fill an adult family home bed?

Listing the vacancy yourself. Medicaid openings list free on marketplaces and private pay listings run about $199 a month with no commission, compared with roughly a full month of care for an agency placement.
