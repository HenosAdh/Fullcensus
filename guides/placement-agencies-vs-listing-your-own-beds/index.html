Placement agencies vs listing your own beds

A placement agency’s standard commission equals your entire first month of care. On a $6,000 room that is $6,000, one time, for one resident. Listing that same bed yourself costs between nothing and $199 a month. Here is the honest math, and the cases where paying the commission is still the right call.

Written by Henos Adhana, Full Census · My family runs an adult family home in Lynnwood · Updated August 2026

The short version

  • A placement agency sends you a family and takes a commission when they move in. The industry standard is roughly one full month of that resident’s care.
  • A marketplace lists your open bed and the family contacts you directly. No commission, ever. Medicaid listings are free and private pay runs about $199 a month.
  • The difference on one resident: around $6,000 versus around $199.
  • But agencies are not a scam. They do real work, they carry the marketing cost, and they only get paid when you actually fill a bed. No risk, high price.
  • The honest rule: use an agency when a bed has sat empty long enough that the lost revenue is bigger than the commission. Use listings and your own presence for everything else.

What a placement agency actually charges

Placement and referral agencies do not bill the family. They bill you, and only when a resident moves in. The standard commission across the industry equals about one month of that resident’s care.

That structure is exactly why the model works so well for them and feels so expensive to you. Nothing is owed until somebody arrives, so there is no risk in trying it. Then the first invoice lands, and it is the largest single marketing cost most owners ever pay.

Commission structures vary by agency and some will negotiate. Always get the fee in writing before you accept a referral, and ask specifically whether it is charged again if the resident leaves and later returns.

What listing the bed yourself costs

The alternative is putting the vacancy where families are already looking, and letting them call you directly. On a marketplace like Room & Care, a Medicaid opening lists free and a private pay opening runs about $199 a month with no contract, cancelled the moment the bed fills. There are no commissions and no placement fees at all.

One resident, both ways. Say a private pay room at $6,000 a month takes you six weeks to fill. Through an agency that is roughly $6,000 in commission. Listing it yourself is about $199 for the first month, plus a second month if it runs long, so call it $400. Same resident, same room, and roughly a fifteenfold difference in what it cost you to find them.

So when is an agency actually worth it?

I am not going to tell you never to use one. An empty bed costs you $5,000 to $8,000 every month it stays empty, and that clock does not care about your principles. Run the comparison honestly:

  • The bed has been empty two months or more. You have already lost more than the commission. Paying it to fill the room this week is simply the better trade.
  • You need a very specific placement. Complex behavioral needs, bariatric care, or a hard-to-match situation where an advisor’s rolodex genuinely beats a listing.
  • You are brand new with no reputation yet. An agency can bridge you to your first two or three residents while your own presence is still being built.
  • You truly cannot handle inquiry calls. Direct listings mean families calling you. If nobody can answer the phone, a listing will not work.

And where it is not worth it: a bed that just opened, in a home families can already find on their own. Paying a full month of care to fill a room that would have filled anyway in three weeks is the most expensive mistake in this business, and it is invisible, because you never get to see the version where you kept the money.

The part nobody says out loud

A placement agency’s incentive is to fill a bed, not necessarily your bed. The advisor talking to that family is choosing among the homes in their network, and the ones that pay reliably and respond fastest tend to get shown first. That is not corruption, it is just how any referral business works. But it does mean you are renting access to families rather than building it.

Anything you rent, you stop having the moment you stop paying. The homes that stay full year after year are the ones where families arrive already knowing the name, because a discharge planner recommended them or because they found the home themselves. That is the thing worth building, and it is why I do what I do.

What to build so you need agencies less

  • List your open beds where families search. Free for Medicaid, cheap for private pay. Start with our plain-English guide to Room & Care.
  • Own your Google Business Profile. Claimed, photos, hours, correct category. It is free and it is the first thing a family checks.
  • Have a website with your name on it. When a discharge planner says your home’s name, the family Googles it that night. If nothing comes up, you were never really in the running.
  • Get listed on a free directory. We list Washington homes at no cost on Dear Care Home, and we never sell anyone’s information.
  • Answer the phone fast. Families mid-discharge move quickly. Response time beats every other advantage you can buy.

One commission costs more than a website.

A single placement fee is roughly a full month of care, often $6,000. A website you own outright costs a fraction of that, once, and it keeps working every month afterwards. That is the whole argument, and it is why I do this.

Common questions

How much does a placement agency charge an adult family home?

The industry standard commission equals roughly one month of the resident’s care, so about $5,000 to $8,000 for a typical Washington private pay room. It is charged once, when the resident moves in, and the family pays nothing.

Do families pay to use a placement service?

No. These services are free to families because the care home pays the commission. That is worth understanding, because it explains why an advisor’s recommendations come from their paying network.

Is it cheaper to list my own beds?

Dramatically, yes. A Medicaid listing on a marketplace is free and private pay runs about $199 a month with no commission, compared with roughly a full month of care for an agency placement. The trade is that you handle the inquiry calls yourself.

Can I use both a placement agency and my own listings?

Yes, and most owners should. List your beds yourself as the default, and keep an agency relationship for rooms that have sat empty long enough that the commission costs less than another month of vacancy.

What is the difference between a placement agency and a marketplace?

A placement agency employs advisors who talk to families and steer them toward homes in their network, then charges the home a commission. A marketplace lists your available room and lets families contact you directly, charging a flat listing fee or nothing at all.
